Ginger Chicken Stir-Fry

Velveted chicken and crisp vegetables in a ginger garlic sauce, cooked in twelve minutes.

Chinese-inspired dinner · Updated 16 October 2026
Prep15 min
Cook12 min
Total27 min
Serves4
DifficultyEasy
Calories398 kcal

Takeaway chicken is tender because of velveting: a few minutes in cornflour and soy before it ever meets the pan, which holds the moisture in and gives the sauce something to cling to. It costs two minutes and is the difference between this and a dry stir-fry. Mix the sauce before you light the hob, because after that nothing waits.

How to make Ginger Chicken Stir-Fry

Ingredients

4

Quantities scale automatically. Cooking times stay the same.

Velveted chicken

Vegetables

Sauce

Equipment

  • Wok or large heavy skillet
  • Two mixing bowls
  • Fine grater

Method

  1. 1

    Velvet the chicken

    Toss the sliced chicken with the cornflour, soy sauce, Shaoxing wine and sesame oil until every piece is coated in a thin slick. Leave it 10 minutes while you prepare everything else.

  2. 2

    Mix the sauce and prep

    Whisk all the sauce ingredients together in a bowl, making sure the cornflour dissolves. Cut all the vegetables and set them by the hob. A stir-fry gives you no time to chop once it starts.

  3. 3

    Sear the chicken

    Heat the oil in the wok over high until it just smokes. Add the chicken in one layer, leave it 90 seconds without touching it, then stir-fry 2 minutes until it is golden outside and barely cooked through. Lift it out onto a plate.

  4. 4

    Cook the hard vegetables

    Add the broccoli and beans to the same pan with 3 tablespoons of water and cover for 2 minutes, so they steam briefly, then uncover and stir-fry 1 minute until bright green and still squeaky.

  5. 5

    Add the aromatics

    Add the pepper, ginger, garlic and spring onion whites and stir-fry 60 seconds. Keep everything moving; ginger and garlic burn in seconds at this heat and turn the whole pan bitter.

  6. 6

    Combine and glaze

    Return the chicken, pour in the sauce and toss for 1 to 2 minutes, until it thickens and coats everything in a glossy layer. Add the spring onion greens and serve immediately with rice.

Cook’s tips

  • Slice the chicken thinly across the grain. Thick pieces need longer than the vegetables can survive.
  • Get the pan hotter than feels sensible and cook in two batches if it is small. A crowded wok boils the chicken in its own juices.
  • Everything chopped and within reach before the oil goes in. Twelve minutes of cooking does not pause for prep.

Variations

  • Swap the chicken for prawns, beef strips or pressed firm tofu, and use hoisin in place of the oyster sauce to keep it vegetarian.
  • Use whatever vegetables are in the fridge: cabbage, carrot, mushrooms, baby corn or bok choy all work in the same order, hardest first.
  • Add a tablespoon of chilli garlic sauce or a drizzle of chilli oil at the end for heat.

Storage and make-ahead

Keeps 3 days in the fridge, though the vegetables soften. Reheat in a hot pan for 2 to 3 minutes rather than the microwave. Freezing is not recommended, as the sauce separates and the vegetables collapse.

Ginger Chicken Stir-Fry: your questions answered

What does velveting actually do?

The cornflour and soy form a thin coating that seals the surface, so the chicken holds its juices through high heat and comes out silky rather than stringy. It is the main reason restaurant stir-fries are more tender.

Can I use a normal frying pan?

Yes, as long as it is heavy and wide. Use the largest you have, get it very hot, and cook in two batches; a thin pan loses its heat the moment the chicken lands.

My sauce never thickened. Why?

Either the cornflour settled at the bottom of the bowl, or the pan was not hot enough to bring the sauce to a boil. Whisk it again before pouring, and let it bubble for a full minute.
