Smoky Tomato Pasta

A pantry pasta built on slow-blistered tomatoes, smoked paprika and a silky, emulsified finish.

Italian-inspired dinner · Updated 28 July 2026
Prep8 min
Cook22 min
Total30 min
Serves4
DifficultyEasy
Calories524 kcal

The trick is patience in one place only: letting the tomatoes properly collapse and catch before anything else goes in. Smoked paprika gives it a depth that tastes like it simmered for an hour, and a knob of butter at the end makes the sauce cling.

Equipment

  • Large pot
  • Wide sauté pan

Method

  1. 1

    Soften the aromatics

    Heat the olive oil in a wide pan over medium. Cook the onion with a pinch of salt for 6-7 minutes until soft and translucent, then add the garlic for 1 minute more.

  2. 2

    Caramelise the paste

    Stir in the tomato paste, smoked paprika and chilli flakes and cook for 2 full minutes, until the paste darkens to a brick red and smells sweet rather than raw. This is where the depth comes from.

  3. 3

    Add the tomatoes

    Tip in the crushed tomatoes and the sugar, season, and simmer uncovered for 12-15 minutes, stirring now and then, until the sauce thickens and the oil separates at the edges.

  4. 4

    Cook the pasta

    Meanwhile boil the pasta in well-salted water until 2 minutes short of the package time. Reserve a large mug of pasta water before draining.

  5. 5

    Marry them

    Add the drained pasta to the sauce with a splash of pasta water and toss over medium heat for 2 minutes so the pasta finishes cooking in the sauce.

  6. 6

    Emulsify and serve

    Off the heat, beat in the butter and parmesan with more pasta water until the sauce turns glossy and coats each tube. Top with basil.

Cook’s tips

  • Cooking the tomato paste until it darkens is the single highest-impact step; two minutes of attention pays for itself.
  • Buy whole plum tomatoes and crush them yourself. Pre-crushed tins are usually made from lower-grade fruit.
  • Finishing the pasta in the sauce, not the water, is what makes restaurant pasta taste different from home pasta.

Variations

  • Add a scoop of ricotta or a splash of cream for a rosy, richer sauce.
  • Fry chorizo or pancetta at the start and build the sauce in the rendered fat.
  • Stir a tin of drained cannellini beans through at the end for protein.

Storage and make-ahead

The sauce keeps 5 days refrigerated and freezes for 3 months, so it is worth doubling. Store sauce and pasta separately if you can.

Smoky Tomato Pasta: your questions answered

Can I use fresh tomatoes?

In peak summer, yes; use about 2 lb of ripe tomatoes, roughly chopped, and simmer 10 minutes longer to drive off the extra water. Out of season, tinned will taste better.

Why is my sauce watery?

It has not reduced enough. Keep it at an active simmer uncovered until you can drag a spoon through and the trail holds for a second.

What pasta shape is best?

Ridged tubes like rigatoni catch this sauce best. Long shapes work but need a slightly looser sauce and more vigorous tossing.
