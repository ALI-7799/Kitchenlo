Classic Shakshuka

Eggs poached in a spiced tomato and pepper sauce, cooked and served in one pan.

Middle Eastern brunch · Updated 27 August 2026
Prep10 min
Cook30 min
Total40 min
Serves4
DifficultyEasy
Calories318 kcal

Shakshuka is a lesson in patience at one point only: the peppers and onions need long enough to turn genuinely sweet before the tomatoes go in. Rush that and the sauce stays sharp. Everything after it takes ten minutes.

Equipment

  • Wide lidded skillet
  • Wooden spoon

Method

  1. 1

    Sweat the vegetables

    Heat the olive oil in a wide skillet over medium. Cook the onion and peppers with a pinch of salt for 12-15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until soft, slumped and sweet. This is the step that decides the dish.

  2. 2

    Bloom the spices

    Add the garlic, cumin, paprika, caraway, cayenne and tomato paste. Cook 2 minutes, stirring constantly, until the paste darkens and the spices smell toasted rather than dusty.

  3. 3

    Simmer the sauce

    Add the crushed tomatoes, sugar and salt. Simmer uncovered for 10-12 minutes until thick enough that a spoon dragged through leaves a trail that holds for a second.

  4. 4

    Make wells

    Taste and adjust the salt now, because you cannot stir it once the eggs are in. Use the back of a spoon to make eight shallow wells in the sauce.

  5. 5

    Add the eggs

    Crack an egg into each well. Season the tops, cover the pan, and cook on low for 6-8 minutes, until the whites are just set and the yolks still wobble.

  6. 6

    Serve from the pan

    Scatter over the feta and herbs and bring the pan to the table with plenty of flatbread for scooping.

Cook’s tips

  • Do not hurry the peppers and onions. Fifteen slow minutes is what makes the sauce taste sweet rather than acidic.
  • Crack each egg into a small cup first, then slide it into its well. It gives you far more control than cracking directly into a hot pan.
  • Take it off the heat when the whites look barely set; carryover heat firms them up while you carry the pan to the table.

Variations

  • Add merguez sausage or chorizo with the onions for a meaty version.
  • Stir a few handfuls of spinach into the sauce before adding the eggs.
  • Make it green with courgette, leek and herbs in place of the tomatoes and peppers.

Storage and make-ahead

The sauce keeps 5 days refrigerated and freezes for 3 months, so it is worth doubling. Store it without the eggs and poach fresh ones into a reheated portion in about six minutes.

Classic Shakshuka: your questions answered

How do I stop the yolks overcooking?

Keep the heat low once the eggs go in and use a lid, so the tops set by trapped steam rather than by the sauce getting hotter. Check at six minutes; the whites should be opaque and the yolks still move when you shake the pan.

Can I make it ahead for a brunch?

Make the sauce entirely ahead, which genuinely improves it. Reheat it to a simmer and poach the eggs just before serving, which takes about eight minutes.

What do I serve with it?

Bread is not optional; the sauce needs something to carry it. Challah, pita, sourdough or any flatbread all work. A sharp green salad cuts the richness well.
