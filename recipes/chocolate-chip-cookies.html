Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies

Thick cookies with crisp edges and a soft centre, made with browned butter and chopped dark chocolate.

American dessert · Updated 16 October 2026
Prep20 min
Cook12 min
Total32 min
Serves18
DifficultyEasy
Calories284 kcal

Browning the butter is ten minutes of work for a flavour you cannot get any other way: toffee and toasted nuts, sitting under the chocolate. The other change worth making is chopping a bar instead of using chips, so you get shards and puddles rather than uniform dots. Chilling the dough is not optional if you want thick cookies.

How to make Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies

Ingredients

18

Quantities scale automatically. Cooking times stay the same.

Dough

Chocolate and finishing

Equipment

  • Light-coloured saucepan
  • Electric whisk or stand mixer
  • Two baking sheets
  • Ice cream scoop, optional

Method

  1. 1

    Brown the butter

    Melt the butter in a light-coloured pan over medium heat, then keep cooking it, swirling often, for 5 to 7 minutes. It will foam, quieten, and then the milk solids at the bottom will turn golden brown and smell nutty. Pour it at once into a bowl, brown bits and all.

  2. 2

    Cool it down

    Leave the butter 15 minutes, until it is warm rather than hot. Hot butter cooks the egg and the cookies spread flat.

  3. 3

    Beat in the sugars

    Whisk both sugars into the butter for a full minute, until the mixture looks like wet sand and is no longer grainy at the edges.

  4. 4

    Add the eggs and vanilla

    Beat in the egg, the extra yolk and the vanilla for 1 to 2 minutes, until the mixture turns slightly paler and thickens enough to ribbon off the whisk.

  5. 5

    Fold in the dry ingredients

    Add the flour, bicarbonate of soda and salt and fold with a spatula until a few floury streaks remain, then add the chopped chocolate and fold just until it is distributed. Stop there; worked dough bakes tough.

  6. 6

    Chill the dough

    Scoop into 18 balls of about 60 g, cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, or overnight. This firms the fat so the cookies rise before they spread, and it lets the flour hydrate, which deepens the flavour.

  7. 7

    Bake

    Heat the oven to 190C / 375F. Bake 6 cookies per sheet, well spaced, for 10 to 12 minutes, until the edges are set and golden and the centres still look underdone and puffy.

  8. 8

    Settle and salt

    Sprinkle with flaky salt as they come out and leave them on the hot sheet for 5 minutes to finish setting, then move them to a rack. They collapse slightly into the right texture as they cool.

Cook’s tips

  • Weigh the dough balls if you can. Even cookies bake evenly, and 60 g is the size that gives a crisp edge with a soft middle.
  • Pull them while the centres look wet. A cookie that looks done in the oven is a hard cookie on the plate.
  • If they spread too much, the butter was too warm or the dough was not chilled long enough. Put the next tray back in the fridge for 20 minutes.

Variations

  • Add 100 g of toasted chopped walnuts or pecans with the chocolate.
  • Swap a third of the dark chocolate for milk or white chocolate for a sweeter cookie.
  • Press a few extra chocolate pieces onto the tops before baking for bakery-style puddles on the surface.

Storage and make-ahead

Keep in an airtight tin for 4 days, with a slice of bread in the tin to hold the softness. Raw dough balls freeze for 3 months and bake straight from frozen with 2 extra minutes, which is the reason to make the full batch.

Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies: your questions answered

Can I bake the dough straight away?

You can, but the cookies will be thinner and the flavour flatter. If you are in a hurry, 30 minutes in the freezer gets you most of the benefit of a long chill.

How do I know when the butter is browned enough?

Go by smell and by the colour of the solids at the bottom of the pan: nutty and amber is right, acrid and very dark has gone too far. Use a pale pan so you can actually see it.

Why one whole egg and an extra yolk?

The extra yolk adds fat and emulsifiers without more water, which gives a chewier centre. Two whole eggs make a cakier cookie.
